# GPU-accelerated / fast free CFD solvers for full-car F1 airflow on one laptop (RTX 4060 Laptop 8 GB)

Research date: 2026-10-26. 17 tool calls (search/fetch). Coverage is uneven: FluidX3D, XLB, OpenLB, lettuce, PyFR and OpenFOAM-GPU were checked against primary sources. Palabos, waLBerla (vehicle use), Sailfish, Taichi-LBM, NVIDIA Warp examples, SU2-GPU details and ProLB were not checked or only partly checked. They are listed under Gaps and not described from memory.

## Q1. Candidate catalogue: license, Windows, GPU, Python, STL, moving ground/wheels, turbulence, memory, speed

### Takeaway
FluidX3D is the only candidate checked here that combines native Windows, NVIDIA consumer GPUs, published bytes/cell and MLUPs tables, STL voxelization, rotating-wheel moving boundaries, Smagorinsky LES, force-on-object functions and built-in rendering. Its license forbids commercial use, so monetized YouTube use is legally unclear. XLB (Apache-2.0) and lettuce (MIT) are permissively licensed Python/GPU LBM codes, but neither shows the same car-ready features, and XLB's GPU wheels are Linux-only.

### Cited Findings

**FluidX3D (Moritz Lehmann / ProjectPhysX), OpenCL C++**
- Described as "Free for non-commercial use". Works on Windows and Linux (C++17), with limited macOS/Android support. — [FluidX3D README](https://github.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D)
- LICENSE grants use "for public research, education or personal use". Clause 2 says "Commercial use is not allowed" and "You may not sell this software ... [or] a product or service whose value derives from the functionality of this software". Clause 3 bans military use and clause 4 bans AI training on the source. Clause 5: if "data or results generated by altered source versions are published, the altered source code must be published as well". — [FluidX3D LICENSE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D/master/LICENSE.md)
- The LICENSE text does not mention videos/images or monetized content (summarizer checked the full text). — [FluidX3D LICENSE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D/master/LICENSE.md)
- README FAQ: companies in CFD/consulting/R&D: "Commercial use is not allowed with the current license". Author: "Maybe I will add the option for a second, commercial license later on." — [FluidX3D README](https://github.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D)
- Memory: D3Q19 without extensions is 93 bytes/cell (FP32/FP32) or 55 bytes/cell (FP32/FP16). The README table gives a max grid for 8 GB of 448³ (FP32/FP32) and 534³ (FP32/FP16). — [FluidX3D README](https://github.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D)
- Published single-GPU MLUPs/s: RTX 4060 (desktop) 1614 (FP32), 3052 (FP16S), 3124 (FP16C). RTX 4070: 2646 / 4548 / 5016. RTX 3060: 2108 / 4070 / 3566. The table has RTX 4070M/4080M/4090M laptop entries but **no RTX 4060 Laptop entry**. — [FluidX3D README](https://github.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D)
- Features: binary .stl import with fast GPU voxelization; "moving mid-grid bounce-back boundaries (moving solid boundaries)"; rotating geometry by re-voxelization (Cessna 172, Bell 222, EDF setups); Smagorinsky-Lilly subgrid LES "to keep simulations with very large Reynolds number stable"; functions for force and torque on objects (`calculate_force_on_object()`); immersed-boundary particles (passive or 2-way coupled); multi-GPU since v3.5. — [FluidX3D README](https://github.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D)
- Stated limits: uniform grid with no adaptive mesh refinement; LBM limited to Mach < 0.3; no transonic flow; results accurate "in all but extreme edge cases" (author's claim). — [FluidX3D README](https://github.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D)
- Mercedes F1 W14 sample setup in `setup.cpp`:
  - Grid: `resolution(float3(1,2,0.5), 4000u)`, where 4000 is the "VRAM occupation in MB". si_u = 100 km/h, lbm_u = 0.075, si_nu = 1.48e-5.
  - **Floor is static**: `if(z==0u) lbm.flags[n]=TYPE_S;`, so the shipped sample has no moving ground.
  - Wheels rotate through voxelization with angular velocity `omega = lbm_u/lbm_radius` ("make wheels rotating"). They are not re-voxelized in the loop.
  - Extensions: FP16S, EQUILIBRIUM_BOUNDARIES, MOVING_BOUNDARIES, SUBGRID, GRAPHICS.
  — [FluidX3D setup.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D/master/src/setup.cpp)
- Ahmed body sample: memory = 10000 MB, si_u = 60 m/s, lbm_u = 0.05, static floor. Extensions: FP16C, FORCE_FIELD, EQUILIBRIUM_BOUNDARIES, SUBGRID. It computes body force / drag coefficient. — [FluidX3D setup.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D/master/src/setup.cpp); [README](https://github.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D)
- Validation shipped with the code: a Stokes drag validation setup was added in v1.2. — [FluidX3D README](https://github.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D)

**XLB (Autodesk), Python on JAX / NVIDIA Warp / Neon**
- Apache-2.0 license (permissive; commercial use allowed). — [XLB GitHub](https://github.com/Autodesk/XLB); [XLB paper arXiv:2311.16080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16080), published in Computer Physics Communications (doi 10.1016/j.cpc.2024.109187)
- Backends: JAX (distributed multi-GPU/TPU), Warp (single GPU), Neon (multi-GPU, single-GPU grid refinement). The Neon wheel is listed only for Linux x86_64/ARM. **No Windows support is stated.** Mac is CPU-only. — [XLB GitHub](https://github.com/Autodesk/XLB)
- Collision/turbulence options: BGK, KBC (D3Q27), Smagorinsky LES. The paper also lists MRT, cumulant and recursive regularized collision. — [XLB GitHub](https://github.com/Autodesk/XLB); [XLB paper v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16080v3)
- Geometry: a "3D mesh voxelizer using trimesh". Examples include **DrivAer in a wind tunnel, KBC, about 317 M cells**, plus a NACA airfoil (about 10 M cells) and a building (about 400 M cells). No Ahmed example. — [XLB GitHub](https://github.com/Autodesk/XLB)
- Boundary conditions in the paper: full/half-way bounce-back, equilibrium BC (used for the moving lid), Zou-He, regularized, Bouzidi curved no-slip, extrapolation outflow. **No named "moving wall" BC.** — [XLB paper v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16080v3)
- Performance: DGX A100, f32, D3Q19 BGK, 1 node / 8 GPUs = 11,448 MLUPS (about 1,431 MLUPS/GPU, derived). Single-GPU desktop numbers appear only in a figure that could not be read. Desktop GPUs lose much more speed in f64; the RTX 6000 Ada is "more than six times" faster in f32/f16 than in f64. — [XLB paper v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16080v3)
- No bytes/cell figure is published. Out-of-core support is "coming soon". — [XLB GitHub](https://github.com/Autodesk/XLB)

**OpenLB (KIT), C++**
- CUDA GPU support since release 1.5. Release 1.7 (Feb 2024) moved many models to GPU-capable operators and added a validated turbulent inlet (vortex method). Release 1.9 added AMD support, giving NVIDIA, AMD and Intel GPU support. — [OpenLB 1.7 release](https://www.openlb.net/news/openlb-release-1-7-available-for-download/); [OpenLB 1.5 release](https://www.openlb.net/news/openlb-release-1-5-available-for-download/); [OpenLB news](https://www.openlb.net/news/page/2/)
- Performance (HPC, not laptop): 1000³ cavity at 42.2 GLUPs on 2 nodes × 4 A100. Turbulent nozzle, 360 M cells on 2 nodes: about 36 GLUPs. — [OpenLB performance](https://www.openlb.net/performance/)
- A 2025 KIT paper presents a wall-modeled LES LBM scheme in OpenLB for complex geometries, scaling to 128 nodes and about 18 billion cells. — [arXiv 2506.21804](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.21804)
- License: **not confirmed** by these sources (see Gaps).

**lettuce, Python/PyTorch**
- MIT license. GPU through PyTorch plus custom native CUDA kernels. Tested with CUDA 12.4 to 13.0. Requires Python ≥ 3.12. Includes a `lettuce benchmark` command. — [lettuce GitHub](https://github.com/lettucecfd/lettuce)
- No published MLUPS numbers. The README does not mention STL, turbulence models, force output or Windows. — [lettuce GitHub](https://github.com/lettucecfd/lettuce); [lettuce paper arXiv:2106.12929](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.12929)

**PyFR (high-order flux reconstruction, not LBM)**
- 3-clause BSD license. Backends: CUDA, HIP, OpenCL, OpenMP, Metal. Aimed at scale-resolving simulation near complex geometry. v2.0.3 ran on up to 1024 MI250X GPUs. — [PyFR v2.0.3 (IBM Research listing)](https://research.ibm.com/publications/pyfr-v203-towards-industrial-adoption-of-scale-resolving-simulations); [PyFR 2014 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.1638)
- Windows: the 2014 paper lists Linux/UNIX only. Current Windows support is unverified. No automotive case study was found. — [PyFR 2014 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.1638)

**OpenFOAM GPU ports (AmgX / PETSc4FOAM)**
- PETSc4FOAM moves only the linear solve to the GPU. AmgX gives up to 9× on the pressure solve on an A100 vs a CPU node, and 7× on a V100 with 8 M cells. — [NVIDIA OpenFOAM 2021 abstract](https://www.esi-group.com/sites/default/files/resource/other/2611/OpenFOAM_Conference_2021_Abstract_Martineau_NVIDIA.pdf); [NVIDIA AmgX OpenFOAM slides](https://www.esi-group.com/sites/default/files/resource/other/1672/8th_OpenFOAM_Conference_NVIDIA_Posey.pdf)
- An academic study found only **up to 10% end-to-end speedup** (2× on the linear system). — [Polimi repository](https://re.public.polimi.it/handle/11311/1249960)

**SU2 GPU**
- A CUDA-enabled SU2 build came from Google Summer of Code 2024 and was described as ongoing work at the Sept 2024 SU2 Conference. No release or speedup figures were found. — [SU2 conference slides (Google Docs)](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1EO5R7XcarBhiqOkh6fK40Qw96EyIvssH/htmlpresent)

**Commercial GPU-LBM (context only, not free)**
- Altair ultraFluidX: a GPU LBM tool for vehicle aerodynamics with Smagorinsky LES plus wall modeling. Needs a CUDA NVIDIA GPU. — [Altair ultraFluidX overview](https://help.altair.com/vwt/topics/getting_started/overview_ultrafluidx_r.htm)
- An SAE 2023 paper compares a GPU LBM against its CPU counterpart on DrivAer. — [SAE 2023-01-0560](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/early-stage-vehicle-aerodynamics-development-using-a-gpu-based-lbm-cfd-solver-2023-01-0560)

### Inferences
- 8 GB / 55 B per cell ≈ 145 M cells (FP16 storage). The README's 534³ ≈ 152 M agrees. A 4:2:1-ish wind-tunnel box at about 145 M cells is roughly 830 × 415 × 415 cells. If a 5.6 m car spans about 40% of the length, cell size is about 1.7 cm. That is coarse for wings and endplates. (Derived from cited bytes/cell; car-fraction assumption is mine.)
- Moving ground in FluidX3D is not in the shipped F1 sample, but the needed parts are listed (MOVING_BOUNDARIES; solid cells with a velocity, as the wheels use). Setting the floor cells' u.y = lbm_u should give a moving belt. This is an engineering inference, not a documented feature.
- Pitch, roll, yaw and ride-height changes per frame mean re-voxelizing the car (FluidX3D does this for rotors in-loop). Each change produces a transient that must wash out before the forces are meaningful. A continuous "lap" run therefore gives quasi-steady forces only if attitude changes slowly relative to the flow-through time.
- For monetized YouTube, FluidX3D clause 2 ("product or service whose value derives from the functionality") is the risk. XLB (Apache-2.0) and lettuce (MIT) carry no such risk.

### Gaps
- Not checked, so no data: Palabos (license, GPU status), waLBerla/lbmpy GPU vehicle cases, Sailfish (believed unmaintained, unverified), Taichi-based LBM, NVIDIA Warp CFD examples, ProLB (commercial), SU2-GPU details.
- OpenLB license text was not confirmed (likely GPL-2, unverified).
- No RTX 4060 **Laptop** MLUPs was published anywhere found. I did not look up the laptop vs desktop 4060 memory-bandwidth figures, which drive LBM speed.
- XLB bytes/cell, single-GPU consumer MLUPs and Windows status are unknown. Possible WSL2 route, untested.

## Q2. Accuracy evidence at laptop-feasible resolution (Ahmed, DrivAer, F1 demos) and Reynolds number

### Takeaway
Hardly any peer-reviewed accuracy data exists for FluidX3D on cars. The F1 evidence is demo-level: a Ferrari SF71H run at about 10 billion cells and Re 3.75 M, reported second-hand, and a W14 sample at 100 km/h. A laptop's roughly 150 M cells is about 70× coarser than that demo, and real F1 Re (~10⁷) is not reachable with resolved walls on 8 GB.

### Cited Findings
- Ferrari SF71H FluidX3D demo by Lehmann: 10 billion voxels, grid 2152×4304×1076, 217k time steps, Re = 3.75 M with Smagorinsky-Lilly; compute plus rendering of 3 × 20 s 4K60 videos took 14 h. This is **second-hand** (a forum repost of a social-media post). The original was not verified, and the GPU hardware was not stated in the repost. — [sg.hu forum repost](https://sg.hu/forum/uzenet/962870403/180824)
- CFD-Online critique of FluidX3D: uniform grid density over the whole domain, single GPU (at the time), no data I/O, and inherent LBM limits. — [CFD Online thread](https://www.cfd-online.com/Forums/main/246049-no-one-talking-about-fluidx3d-post839126.html)
- FluidX3D Concorde: 40 billion cells, 33 h on 32 × AMD MI210 (29 h compute for 67,268 steps + 4 h rendering). — [3dtested](https://www.3dtested.com/news/32-amd-mi210-gpus-render-massive-simulation-33-hours-for-one-second-of-concorde-landing)
- XLB validation is on canonical cases only:
  - 2D cylinder Re 100: Cd_max 3.25 (ref 3.22–3.24), Cl_max 0.985 (ref 0.99–1.01), St 0.300 (ref 0.295–0.305).
  - 3D cavity up to Re 10,000.
  - Re_τ 180 channel LES with KBC.
  - **No car validation in the paper.**
  — [XLB paper v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16080v3)
- XLB f32/f32 results are "unreliable at high resolution without renormalization". This precision caveat is relevant to FP16 storage choices. — [XLB paper v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16080v3)
- Ahmed-body CFD in general is sensitive to mesh and slant angle. An AhmedML study found its OpenFOAM drag at 25° slant moved further from experiment with refinement and that the rear-slant shear layer was missed. — [AhmedML arXiv 2407.20801](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.20801)
- Production vehicle LBM (ultraFluidX) uses LES **with wall modeling**. The FluidX3D README does not mention a wall model. — [Altair ultraFluidX](https://help.altair.com/vwt/topics/getting_started/overview_ultrafluidx_r.htm); [FluidX3D README](https://github.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D)

### Inferences
- With no wall model and stair-step bounce-back at about 1–2 cm cells, boundary layers, ground-effect underfloor flow and wing loading will not be resolved. Downforce magnitudes should be treated as qualitative, and trends (yaw, ride height) as plausible but unvalidated. Wake and vortex visuals will look realistic; absolute Cl and Cd are not trustworthy without calibration against known values.
- Effective Re in such LBM runs is set by the chosen lattice viscosity. The Smagorinsky model stabilizes "high Re" runs, but near-wall physics at Re 10⁷ is not captured.

### Gaps
- No FluidX3D Ahmed-body Cd vs experiment was found. No peer-reviewed FluidX3D external-aero validation was found.
- No published accuracy for any free LBM on a DrivAer or F1 geometry at about 100–150 M cells.

## Q3. Outputs: forces and velocity fields for streamlines/trails

### Takeaway
FluidX3D has force/torque-on-object functions, built-in streamline/particle rendering, and IB particles. XLB and lettuce are Python/array based, so fields are directly available as GPU arrays for custom export.

### Cited Findings
- FluidX3D: `calculate_force_on_object()` and torque functions; the Ahmed setup computes drag coefficient; passive IB particles; interactive/offscreen graphics. — [FluidX3D README](https://github.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D); [setup.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D/master/src/setup.cpp)
- CFD-Online critique cites limited data I/O (at the time of the thread). — [CFD Online](https://www.cfd-online.com/Forums/main/246049-no-one-talking-about-fluidx3d-post839126.html)
- XLB is JAX/Warp array based and differentiable. lettuce is PyTorch tensor based. — [XLB GitHub](https://github.com/Autodesk/XLB); [lettuce GitHub](https://github.com/lettucecfd/lettuce)

### Inferences
- In FluidX3D, downforce = the vertical component of the force on car-flagged cells (TYPE_X). Rendering trails top-down with a locked camera fits its built-in graphics.

### Gaps
- XLB's force (momentum-exchange) API is not documented on the README.

## Q4. Thermal/power limiting on the laptop and runtime scaling

### Takeaway
On laptop RTX 40-series GPUs, `nvidia-smi -pl` is often refused ("not supported") because power is managed by Dynamic Boost and vendor firmware. Throttling must come from the vendor tool or profile, or from capping clocks. LBM runtime scales linearly with cells × steps.

### Cited Findings
- On a laptop RTX 4060, `nvidia-smi -pl 115` returned "Changing power management limit is not supported". The fix in that thread was enabling `nvidia-powerd` and the performance profile. — [EndeavourOS forum](https://forum.endeavouros.com/t/lenovo-loq-15arp9-stuck-at-35w-or-55w-tpg/78348)
- `nvidia-powerd` (Dynamic Boost) decides the laptop GPU power allocation. — [NVIDIA dev forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/the-default-power-limit-of-my-4060-laptop-halves-its-performance/294699)
- Firmware-locked limits persisted even on Windows on some laptops ("not supported in current scope"). — [WinRAID](https://winraid.level1techs.com/t/problem-gpu-power-limit-on-tongfang-gm5pd5e/118020)
- Dell G15 RTX 4060: the limit dropped from about 140 W to 45 W mid-load at 73 °C (EC/firmware behavior). — [Dell community](https://www.dell.com/community/en/conversations/inspiron/g15-5530-rtx-4060-gpu-hard-latches-to-45-w-mid-game-at-73-c-all-kb-steps-exhausted-requesting-i-rev-corrective-ec-firmware/6a4b02b550a08a1eb3665f92)
- Use `nvidia-smi -q -d POWER` to see the default, min and max limits. Desktop users cap power with `nvidia-smi -i 0 -pl 80`. — [NVIDIA dev forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/how-to-limit-the-power-of-3060ti-to-less-than-100-watts/248592)

### Inferences
- LBM is memory-bandwidth bound, so capping core clocks or power (vendor tool such as MSI Center "silent/balanced", or `nvidia-smi -lgc` if supported) should cost less speed than in compute-bound work. This is unverified; measure it.
- Runtime ≈ cells × steps / MLUPs. At 145 M cells and an assumed about 2,500–3,000 MLUPs (desktop-4060 FP16 class; the laptop figure is unpublished), that is roughly 17–21 steps/s. One flow-through over 830 cells at lbm_u 0.075 ≈ 11k steps ≈ 9–11 min.

### Gaps
- No source on MSI Raider GE68HX GPU power behavior, or on whether `-pl`/`-lgc` works on this model under Windows.

## Q5. Cost of a one-off sweep (N yaw/ride-height cases)

### Takeaway
No source gives sweep timings for a laptop. Estimates can only come from cell count × steps ÷ MLUPs (inference below).

### Cited Findings
- Reference scale: 10 B cells × 217k steps took 14 h including rendering (second-hand). — [sg.hu repost](https://sg.hu/forum/uzenet/962870403/180824)
- RTX 4060 (desktop) FluidX3D FP16S: 3052 MLUPs. — [FluidX3D README](https://github.com/ProjectPhysX/FluidX3D)

### Inferences
- Assume about 3 flow-throughs to settle the wake plus 2 to average forces. That is 5 × about 10 min ≈ 50 min per case at 145 M cells, or about 12–15 min per case at about 40 M cells (coarser, and proportionally fewer steps since there are fewer cells per length). For example, 3 yaw × 4 ride heights = 12 cases ≈ 10 h at full resolution, or ≈ 3 h coarse. These are order-of-magnitude estimates; FP16 laptop MLUPs must be measured with FluidX3D's benchmark.
- A lookup-table approach (precompute a sweep once, interpolate per lap frame) avoids a per-frame CFD solve. Per-frame transient CFD over a 90 s lap at real time scale is not feasible on one laptop. Reason: physical time per step = cell size × lbm_u / U; at 1.7 cm and 90 m/s that is about 1.4e-5 s, so a 90 s lap ≈ 6.4 M steps ≈ 3.5–4 days at about 20 steps/s.

### Gaps
- No measured RTX 4060 Laptop FluidX3D/XLB timings exist in the sources; benchmark locally.
