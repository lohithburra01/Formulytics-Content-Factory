# What a race car's wake and wing-tip vortices look like in a corner (research notes, 2026-10-26)

Question: when the car turns, do the airflow trails stay straight behind it, bend with the car, or follow the path? These are short notes. Each claim has a source URL. Where something is my own reasoning or not verified, it is marked **[UNVERIFIED]** or **[REASONING]**.

## Short answer
- **Ground frame, which is what a top-down track video shows:** in still air the wake stays roughly where it was shed. So the trail lies **along the path the car drove**, and each wing tip leaves its own offset curve. After that the trail moves only slowly: it drifts a little toward the **outside** of the corner, it spreads, it rises or sinks, it moves with any wind, and it decays. It does not stay as a straight rod fixed behind the car. It also does not swing around with the car's body.
- **Car-fixed frame, which is what CFD of a cornering car shows:** the oncoming flow is curved. The wake therefore bends along the arc of the corner. In these studies the wake is asymmetric, and the vortex cores move sideways and up or down. That movement grows with distance behind the car "as the structures align with the freestream" (Keogh et al. 2015).

## 2. Simulation evidence (cornering / curved flow)
- **Keogh, Doig, Diasinos, Barber (2015)**, Proc IMechE Part D, "The influence of cornering on the vortical wake structures of an inverted wing". An 80% scale front wing in steady cornering, radius 60 m down to 7.5 m. The wake becomes **highly asymmetric**. The primary and secondary vortices differ in strength, and their cores shift vertically and spanwise. The shift **grows downstream as the structures align with the (curved) freestream**. This shows the vortices follow the curved flow, not the car's straight-back axis. https://digitalcommons.calpoly.edu/aero_fac/134 , DOI https://doi.org/10.1177/0954407015571673 (PDF https://digitalcommons.calpoly.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1134&context=aero_fac returned 403 to me, so the direction of the spanwise shift is **[UNVERIFIED]**)
- **Keogh et al. (2016)**, JWEIA 154:34-46, "The aerodynamic effects on a cornering Ahmed body". Wall-resolved LES at three corner radii. The **outboard C-pillar vortex grows and the inboard one shrinks**, and separation is asymmetric. At a 5-car-length radius drag is +19.2%, and the side force points toward the corner centre. https://dx.doi.org/10.1016/j.jweia.2016.04.002 , https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/the-aerodynamic-effects-on-a-cornering-ahmed-body/
- **Keogh et al., FISITA 2014.** DES of a cornering Ahmed body. The time-averaged near wake changes a lot: the longitudinal vortices change in size, strength and **trajectory**. https://go.fisita.com/store/papers/FISITA2014/F2014-MVC-012
- **Keogh et al., multi-element wing in ground effect, cornering (Fluids 2021, 6(1):3).** https://www.mdpi.com/2311-5521/6/1/3 (403 to me; contents **[UNVERIFIED]**)
- **Rijns, Teschner, Blackburn, Brighton (Cranfield), Physics of Fluids 36, 045119 (2024).** DrivAer hp-F with rear wing, using an MRF plus overset mesh, validated against a moving-ground tunnel. At a radius of 2.9 car lengths downforce is -20% and drag +35%. The drag comes from a **recirculation wake vortex behind the vehicle's inboard side**. DOI https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0204204 , https://dspace.lib.cranfield.ac.uk/handle/1826/21252
- **Chalmers (DrivAer cornering, MRF).** Radius 100-800 m, including roll, slip and steer. https://research.chalmers.se/publication/503721/file/503721_Fulltext.pdf (I could not read the figures)
- **Carreira (IST Lisbon).** Formula Student FST10e cornering CFD in STAR-CCM+ (roll, steer, yaw), validated on track. https://fenix.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/downloadFile/845043405586592/MiguelCarreira_ExtendedAbstract.pdf
- **F1 / FIA 2021-22 rules CFD.** F1 ran wake-following CFD, including a side-by-side Silverstone (Hamilton-Verstappen) case. The cars were designed to throw the wake **upward** so the following car drives under it. I found no public cornering wake figure. https://amazon.science/latest-news/the-science-behind-the-next-gen-2022-F1-car , https://www.autosport.com/all/news/symonds-f1-has-made-a-breakthrough-with-2022-cars/8920589/ , https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article.first-look-formula-1s-2021-car-in-the-wind-tunnel.6ye3S7Pb8NRX1K7PjTBxtS.html
- How CFD is set up: cornering is simulated with a moving or rotating reference frame, with the car fixed in a frame that rotates about the corner centre. https://innovationspace.ansys.com/forum/?p=454547 . Seen from the car, the incoming air is therefore a solid-body rotation, a curved freestream (Keogh 2015 abstract).

## 3. Physics: where the wake goes when the vehicle follows a curve
- **[REASONING, standard kinematics]** Air that is at rest in the ground frame, seen from a frame rotating with the car about the corner centre, moves on circles about that centre. So a passive marker shed at a wing tip traces the **arc of the path at that tip's radius**. The outer tip traces a larger arc than the inner tip.
- **[REASONING]** Wake air has been dragged forward a little by the car (the momentum deficit). Once it is shed, it moves in a straight line along the tangent at the point where it was shed. A tangent to a circle leaves the circle toward the outside, so the trail **slowly peels toward the outside of the corner**, relative to the line the car drove. The effect is small because the wake velocities are small and decay fast. Ground effect also makes a vortex pair near a surface move apart sideways and change height (water-tank 747/DC-10 study: "ground plane caused modifications in the vortex trajectories" https://trid.trb.org/View/91498).
- **Aircraft analogue.** Contrails and wake trails of aircraft in holding patterns lie along the curved ground track, forming loops or racetracks, and then the whole pattern drifts with the wind. NOAA/CIMSS satellite image of curved contrails from two jets holding south of Dallas/Fort Worth (Mar 2026): https://cimss.ssec.wisc.edu/satellite-blog/archives/69463 . Racetrack-contrail explanation: "fly straight for a minute, then turn 180 degrees ... then fly straight again", with wind displacing older segments: https://contrailscience.com/?p=70
- Visible F-15C wingtip vortices in a high-bank turn at the Mach Loop (the trails come off the tips and stream back along the flight path): https://www.flowvis.org/?p=41626 . For general condensation in vortex cores, humidity and loading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wingtip_vortices , https://howthingsfly.si.edu/node/109
- DLR LES shows real vortex pairs deform and curve as they decay, which affects encounter hazard. These papers are about vortex deformation, not turning flight. https://elib.dlr.de/48976 , https://core-cms.cambridgecore.org/core/journals/aeronautical-journal/article/flight-simulator-study-on-the-influence-of-vortex-curvature-on-wake-encounter-hazard-using-les-wind-fields/E18106D3549CF4830D8EF6FF6ADC6CAF

## Implications for the top-down video **[REASONING]**
1. Emit particles at the wing tips (rear-wing tips, front-wing outer tips, around the tyres). Once emitted, keep them **fixed in world (track) coordinates**. Do not attach the trails rigidly to the car frame. The trails will then lie along the racing line, and each tip will trace its own offset curve: the outer tip arc is longer and sits farther out.
2. Add a small drift toward the outside of the corner (the tangent velocity of the dragged air), slow sideways spreading and growth in thickness, and a fade or decay over roughly 0.5-2 s. Through a corner the inner trail should be a little stronger or more chaotic, because of the inboard recirculation found by Rijns 2024 and the outboard/inboard asymmetry found by Keogh 2016. That asymmetry is a secondary detail.
3. Drawing trails as straight lines out of the car's tail, or bending them with the car's yaw, would be physically wrong.
